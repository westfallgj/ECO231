DEMAND of Goods and Services

DEMAND - the amount of some good or service consumers are willing and able to purchase at each price. Any price/all prices, the entire DEMAND Curve

Quantity DEMAND - the total number of units of a good or service consumers are willing to purchase at a given price. Specific/exact, one Point on hte DEMAND Curve.

Law of DEMAND - the inverse relationship between price and Quantity DEMAND
    keeping all other variables that affect DEMAND constant
    • if price goes UP, then Quantity DEMANDED goes DOWN
    • if price goes DOWN, then Quantity DEMANDED goes UP

There are two ways to describe the same relationship between price and Quantity DEMAND: DEMAND Curve and DEMAND Schedule

DEMAND Curve: shows the relationship between price and Quantity DEMANDED on a graph.

DEMAND Schedule - a table that shows a range of prices for a certain good or service and the Quantity DEMANDED at each price

A shift in DEMAND happens when a change in some economic factor (other than price) causes a different Quantity to be DEMANDED at every price.

SUPPLY of Goods and Services

SUPPLY - the amount of some good or service a producer is willing to SUPPLY at each price. Any price/all prices, the entire SUPPLY Curve

Quantity SUPPLIED - the total number of units of a good or service producers are willing to sell at a given price. Specific/exact, one Point on hte SUPPLY Curve.

Law of SUPPLY - assuming all other variables that affect SUPPLY are held constant
    • if price goes UP, then Quantity SUPPLIED goes UP
    • if price goes DOWN, then Quantity SUPPLIED goes DOWN 

SUPPLY Schedule - a table that shows a range of prices for a certain good or service and the Quantity SUPPLIED at each price.

Shift in SUPPLY - when a change in some economic factor (other than price) causes a different Quantity to be SUPPLIED at every price.

Factors that affect SUPPLY:
    • Input prices - the costs of labor, materials, and machinery that are used to produce goods and services.
    • Natural conditions
    • Technology
    • Government policies

When the cost of production increases, the SUPPLY Curve shifts LEFT to a new price level.

EQUILIBRIUM - the combination of price and Quantity where there is no economic pressure from surpluses or shortages that would cause price or Quantity to change
    
    Quantity DEMANDED = Quantity SUPPLIED

EQUILIBRIUM Price - the price where Quantity DEMANDED is equal to Quantity SUPPLIED

EQUILIBRIUM Quantity - the Quantity at which Quantity DEMANDED and Quantity SUPPLIED are equal for a certain price level.

Surplus or Excess SUPPLY - at the existing price, Quantity SUPPLIED exceeds the Quantity DEMANDED. Above the EQUILIBRIUM Point

Shortage or Excess DEMAND - at the existing price, the Quantity DEMANDED exceeds the Quantity SUPPLIED. Below the EQUILIBRIUM Point

The DEMAND curve (D) and the SUPPLY curve (S) intersect at the EQUILIBRIUM Point E.

The EQUILIBRIUM price is the only price where Quantity DEMANDED = Quantity SUPPLIED

At a price above EQUILIBRIUM, Quantity DEMANDED > Quantity DEMANDED, so there is
excess SUPPLY.

At a price below EQUILIBRIUM, Quantity DEMANDED > Quantity DEMANDED, so there is
excess DEMAND.

Factors that increase SUPPLY
    Favorable natural conditions for production
    Fall in input prices
    Improved Technology
    Lower product taxes/less costly regulations

Factors that decrease SUPPLY
    Poor natural conditions for production
    Rise in input prices
    Decline Technology
    Higher product taxes/more costly regulations

Shift in SUPPLY means a change in the Quantity Supplied at every price. The entire Supply Curve moves for all points.

Changes in EQUILIBRIUM Price and Quantity: The Four-Step Process

Four-step process to determining how an economic event affects EQUILIBRIUM price
and Quantity:

    • Step 1. Draw a DEMAND and SUPPLY model before the economic change took place.
        Requires four standard pieces of information: 
            The Law of DEMAND (which tells us the slope or the DEMAND Curve)
            The Law of SUPPLY (whiich gives us the sloop of the SUPPLY Curve)
            The shift in variables of DEMAND
            The shift in variables of SUPPLY
    • Step 2. Decide whether the economic change affects DEMAND or SUPPLY.
        Is it from the list of DEMAND factors of SUPPLY factors
    • Step 3. Decide whether the effect causes a curve shift to the right or to the left, and sketch the new curve on the diagram.
    • Step 4. Identify the new EQUILIBRIUM and then compare to the original.

Price Ceilings and Price Floors

Price Controls: laws the government enacts to regulate prices
Price Ceiling: keeps price from rising above a certain level, the legal maximum price that one pays for a good or service.
Price Floor: keeps prics from sinking below a certain level, the legal minimum price that one pays for a good of service.
